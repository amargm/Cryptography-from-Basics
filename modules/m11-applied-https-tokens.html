Applied HTTPS, Tokens & E2EE

Now we watch every individual puzzle piece—symmetric AES, Diffie-Hellman, SHA-256 hashes, digital signatures, and Base64—snap together into the tools you use every single day.

1. The Anatomy of an HTTPS Connection

Every time you see the padlock icon in your browser, your device executes a 4-act cryptographic symphony in less than 100 milliseconds:

The TLS 1.3 Handshake Recipe

Act 1 (Trust): The website sends its X.509 digital certificate. Your browser checks the signature against its built-in Root CA store to ensure you are talking to the genuine site.
Act 2 (Key Agreement): Using Diffie-Hellman / Elliptic Curves, your browser and the server agree on a fresh, temporary shared symmetric key.
Act 3 (Bulk Encryption): Both sides switch to lightning-fast AES-GCM symmetric encryption.
Act 4 (Integrity): Every web page, image, and form submit is verified with an HMAC/GCM authentication tag so no Wi-Fi hacker can modify your data.

2. Inside a JSON Web Token (JWT)

When you log into a modern website, the server often issues a JWT token stored in your browser cookies.

The Green Part is the Signature: The server takes the Header + Payload and signs them with its private secret key. If a user tampers with their role from "user" to "admin", the signature breaks instantly!

3. End-to-End Encryption & Passkeys

In traditional web apps, the server can read your data. In End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) (WhatsApp, Signal):

  • Your message is encrypted directly on your phone using Bob's public key.
  • The WhatsApp servers only route encrypted mathematical noise; they do not hold Bob's private key.
  • Even if law enforcement issues a warrant to WhatsApp, WhatsApp physically cannot decrypt your messages.

Passkeys (FIDO2 / WebAuthn): The modern death of passwords. Instead of typing a password that can be phished, your phone's biometric enclave generates a unique asymmetric key pair for each website. Logging in simply signs a challenge using your private key via fingerprint or FaceID!
