The Public-Key Revolution

For 2,000 years, secure communication required a shared secret. If Alice and Bob never met in person, how could they start? In 1976, mathematics solved this impossible paradox.

1. The Padlock Mental Model

In symmetric cryptography, Alice and Bob have identical keys. But in Asymmetric Cryptography, every person has a pair of two mathematically connected keys:

  • Public Key: An open padlock. You can manufacture a million copies and hand them out to everyone on the street. Anyone can drop a letter in a box and snap your padlock shut.
  • Private Key: The only physical key that opens that padlock. You keep it locked in your pocket and never show it to anyone.

Now, a complete stranger across the globe can take your public padlock, lock their credit card info inside, and send it to you. Even if Eve intercepts the box, she has no private key to open it!

3. The Engineering Compromise: Hybrid Encryption

If asymmetric cryptography is so wonderful, why don't we use it to encrypt everything?

Speed vs. Key Exchange

Asymmetric math (RSA and Elliptic Curves) is roughly 1,000 times slower than symmetric AES. Encrypting a 2-hour 4K movie with RSA would cause your smartphone to overheat and drain the battery.

The Solution (Hybrid Cryptography): Use asymmetric keys for 0.05 seconds to safely exchange a fresh, random AES symmetric key. Then, switch to lightning-fast AES for the rest of the session!
